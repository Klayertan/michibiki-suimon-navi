Paddy water tube

A pipe in the soil, a float on the water inside it, and a laser distance sensor in the cap. This page shows how it reads the water level and how it goes together. Draft design, 2026-10-13: not yet built or tested.

How it reads the water

Holes in the buried half of the pipe let water in, so the level inside matches the field, even when it has dropped below the soil. The sensor measures down to the float.

level = H − d − f   (H 200 mm sensor above soil, f 8 mm float above water)

Below about −190 mm the float rests on the bottom of the pipe. The sensor then reports no reading. It never reports zero for a missing value.

How it goes together

  1. Drill 5 mm holes round the lower 200 mm of a 400 mm pipe. Wrap that half in fine mesh.
  2. Push the pipe 200 mm into the soil and scoop the mud out from inside.
  3. Drop the float in, flat face up.
  4. Fit the cap and tighten the screw in its side. Grooves in the cap let the pipe breathe.
  5. Lay the sensor face down over the window and screw the lid on.
  6. Run the cable to the controller on a stake.
  7. Calibrate: measure from the sensor to the soil once, then check against the staff gauge.

Printed parts: cap, lid and float, in PETG. The pipe is ordinary 100 mm PVC drain pipe.
